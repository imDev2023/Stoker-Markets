## The ADRs

`docs/adr/` - fee model and the Creator Forward (0009, which supersedes 0007 and 0001: 1.00% Platform Fee and 0% Creator Fee on-chain, half of what the platform collects on a coin's trades forwarded to its Creator on the curve and after Graduation), Cloudflare hosting (0002), server-built transactions (0003), key custody (0004), price-banded curve rules (0005), Reward Rounds and Burn budgets (0006, amended by #43's Payout Asset and by 0019), the Platform Net and the Budget Table (0019, which amends 0006: the Burn's and the Flywheel's shares are rows of one versioned table), the Creator Ladder (0020, which amends 0009's half and supersedes 0019's tier table: a graduated coin's Creator Tier, climbed by Active Days, sets the Creator's share of its pool fees), Creator Earnings Destinations (0011, which amends 0009 and 0006: a Creator points a coin's earnings at their wallet, its holders, a buyback and burn, or locked liquidity, fixed at launch, each coin with a rule having a pot of its own), the Creator Lock (0023, which amends 0005: where a Quote Asset allows a lock its rule groups carry ranges for the sale and the lock, and the Indexer holds a coin with a lock to the launch shape).

## Chain and launches

- LaunchLab checks a trade's `minimum_amount_out` against what reaches the wallet after the received asset's Token-2022 transfer fee (proven on the fork with a Reward Launch's coin and the taxed quote): quote limits from the wallet-side figure, never the pre-fee one, or the program fails with 6004.
- A Reward Launch (#16) is `initialize_with_token_2022` with a transfer fee at 1%, 2%, 3%, 4% or 5% (2% and 4% added by the owner on 2026-11-07, #172); the fee-config authority is LaunchLab's vault authority until migration, then the operations wallet, which the `reward-tax-lock` job revokes after Graduation so the tax can never change; the withdraw-withheld authority is the operations wallet from launch.
  Curve rule group 0 pins standard coins, group 1 Reward Launches.
- The launch shape (owner, 2026-11-02, #166, ADR 0005 amended): every new coin is 1,000,000,000,000 at 6 decimals (1e18 raw) with 793,100,000,000 sold, `LAUNCH_SHAPE` in `packages/chain/src/shape.ts`, the 1B shape's 79.31% sold, so targets and market caps keep their figures and a coin's price is a thousandth of a 1B coin's.
  Coins launched on the 1B shape (devnet's) keep it: the decoder accepts `EARLIER_LAUNCH_SHAPES`, the API divides by each coin's own supply, and a rule group that still pins 1B reads as no band until the Quote Registry rewrites it.
  The Reward Rounds' rules stay absolute coin counts (ADR 0006), so on a 1T coin the smallest buy clears the 10,000-coin minimum: a test that wants a holder under it hands the coins over by transfer.
  LaunchLab panics (`constant_product.rs` 64:14) on a launch that sells under half its supply.
  In the web `COIN_SUPPLY` (`src/lib/format.ts`) is the launch form's figure alone (the Supply row, the First Buy's and the airdrop's shares); anything about a launched coin reads the API's figures (a launch summary carries the coin's own `supply`, which the Advanced Charts datafeed multiplies its market-cap series by; the chart's head shows `market.marketCap`; the Stats page's burned share adds the burns back to the holders' supply), so a 1B coin keeps its own.
- The Creator Lock (owner, 2026-11-13, #186, ADR 0023, three answers after the proof on the local chain; the chain's side is built, the form, the mark and the claim from the page are not): coins LaunchLab holds for the Creator, taken out of the curve's sale so the pool keeps its 20.69% and the Graduation market cap is unchanged; a fifth of the supply at most; at least seven days from the curve selling out until the last coin unlocks (the owner's own answer, "can we start by giving options of 7 days, 30 days etc?", read as a 7-day floor with the form's steps above it); all of it given to the Creator's wallet in the launch transaction.
  A curve rule cannot hold the sale and the lock to one sum, so where a Quote Asset allows a lock its two groups carry ranges (one constraint more, no new group) and the Indexer holds the coin to the shape; the Quote Registry writes those ranges only from the second half on.
  The clock starts at the buy that sells the curve out, a claim needs the migration, and a Reward Launch's Creator pays the Reward Tax on what they claim.
  Three calls of mine are the owner's to overrule: a cliff and an unlock time of four years each at most; any lock size up to the cap is accepted from the chain, whatever steps the form offers; the Indexer lists no coin with a lock until the second half lands.
- Copy Protection (owner, 2026-10-26, #120 item 1, ADR 0012): for seven days after a coin launches (`COPY_PROTECTION_SECONDS`, which may become thirty) nobody else may launch a coin with its name, ticker or image; afterwards anyone may and the coin gets a label naming the original.
  Near copies (one edit from five characters, BABY or MINI in front) are refused inside the window too; tickers stay ASCII.
  Not decided: a Quote Asset's own ticker (refuse always, proposed) and Jupiter-verified tickers (label only, proposed).
- Referral share fee (#21): a trade names `share_fee_rate` and the referrer's Quote Asset account (the account before the system program among the remaining accounts); the rate is capped by the GlobalConfig's `max_share_fee_rate` (SOL 1%, most configs including every stock 0), so `referralShare` on a Quote Asset says whether it applies.
  The Platform Fee and Creator Fee shown anywhere come from the chain (`GET /v1/platform`), never a constant; a change is announced ahead (#22) and applied by the cold admin key.
- Only Raydium's migrate wallet can graduate a pool, and its crank migrates a new platform's pools within seconds (proven on real devnet 2026-09-27).
- A new Quote Asset needs Raydium to create its GlobalConfig; only the Platform Token's is planned (after it graduates).
- Permanent metadata (ADR 0008, #44): every coin's image and metadata JSON are Arweave data items signed with the operations key at prepare time (so the launch transaction carries `ARWEAVE_GATEWAY_URL/<id>`, `https://arweave.net` on mainnet, and a retry keeps it) and posted through ArDrive Turbo's HTTP API only once the launch is indexed; Turbo holds items by id, so a repeat post is free.
  Never Irys (its Arweave endpoints retire on 2026-11-01).
  R2 plus Postgres serve the same document under our own domain as the cache, never as the on-chain address; the API refuses a temporary gateway or `PUBLIC_API_URL` on mainnet.
  The SDK runs in a Worker but is not used (`spike/turbo-workers`).

## Fees and Creator Earnings

- Creator earnings (ADR 0009, #85, owner 2026-10-08): StonkFun's split, 1.00% Platform Fee and 0% Creator Fee on the curve; the Creator's share is half of what the platform collects on their coin's trades on either venue (half of each curve trade's Platform Fee, half of each pool creator fee collection, each rounded down on its own), forwarded in the Quote Asset, a Reward Launch like any coin, once worth `CREATOR_FORWARD_MIN_LAMPORTS`; nothing to claim, no claim UI or endpoint, and the Indexer tracks no claims.
  A share is owed only once its fee is collected (a curve trade when a Platform Fee claim for its Quote Asset landed at or after its slot), and what is owed counts every forward that is `sending` or `paid`, so nothing is paid twice.
  The user-facing name is Creator Earnings (`GET /v1/creators/:address/earnings`); "Creator Fee" is LaunchLab's on-chain term only, which the fee-change flow still names.
  The creator page is the connected wallet's own (owner, 2026-10-08): `/creator` only, no nav or footer entry, reached from the account menu once the wallet has launched a coin, and nothing links to a Creator's page (the Ledger shows each forward's wallet on the explorer).
- The Creator Ladder (#188, ADR 0020, owner 2026-11-10, answered before the build): three Creator Tiers, 50% on the curve and after Graduation ("50% during and after graduation and 60% after 15 days and 70% after 30", which replaced ADR 0019's 60% from the Graduation itself), the second and the top reached by Active Days, the measure the owner picked over a holder count and over age with total volume: a UTC day on which the coin's pool traded at least 1% of its own Graduation Target (`activeDayFloorBps`), days that need not follow one another.
  The 15 and the 30 are read as Active Days; calendar days, if the owner meant those, are a new rule, not a new model.
  A tier is the coin's, never the Creator's across their coins; it is recorded once (a `creator_tiers` row with the chain's slot, written by the Fee Collector before it collects), applies to the pool fees collected after that slot under the ladder version in force at the collection's block time, and never falls, whatever a later version asks.
  Earlier coins are on the same ladder with the days they have.
  The count is stored (`graduations.active_days`, hourly) because the coin page reads its coin every four seconds; a coin on the top tier is no longer counted.
  The ladder is versioned and announced like the Budget Table; version 1 is in the migration, in force from the first lamport, which backdates nothing since no coin had a tier.
  A share shows on `/fees` and `/announcements` alone; the coin page and `/creator` name the tier and count the days.
  Copy that says "half" about what every coin starts with (the landing, the launch form, the share card, the hero's lede) stays until the owner picks the pitch's wording for the ladder.
- Creator Earnings Destinations (#113 with #184's Creator half, ADR 0011, built 2026-11-10 on the owner's word to take the unit): four, `wallet` (the default, Creator Forwards), `holders` (Holder Dividends through Reward Rounds of source `earnings`, in the Quote Asset, any coin, no staking), `burn` (a Creator Buyback on the curve too, burning exactly what it bought) and `liquidity` (after Graduation: a buy, a deposit and a lock in one transaction, the platform keeping the new position's Fee Key and paying its rent), chosen on the launch form, kept on the draft, copied to the Launch by the Indexer and never changed; any launch type goes with any destination.
  A coin with a rule has a pot of its own (collected share, less its Graduation Fee, less what the rule applied), and a Creator Forward is owed only on the coins that pay the wallet.
  Three calls the session made and told the owner, his to overrule: before Graduation the liquidity share waits in the pot and is never spent on the curve; the form's choice is a "Creator earnings" section of its own and the "Holder rewards, Coming soon" launch type is gone; an addition needs the pot worth `LIQUIDITY_ADD_MIN_LAMPORTS` (0.25 SOL on mainnet), since every addition is a locked position of its own (the lock program cannot add to one).
  Burning the LP tokens instead of locking them was considered and not done: the owner's decision of 2026-11-09 is locked liquidity with the Fee Key.
  Nothing is trustless: the Creator Forward job applies a rule, every action on the Ledger, and the coin's card says so.
- The Graduation Fee (owner, 2026-10-30, ADR 0015, #157): `GRADUATION_FEE_LAMPORTS` (0.015 SOL on mainnet, 0.001 on the test stack) is fixed on the `graduations` row as the migration is indexed and withheld from the Creator's share on that coin, in the coin's Quote Asset at the Quote Registry's price at the time, by `withholdGraduationFees` (`apps/api/src/fees/graduationFee.ts`) before the Fee Collector's sweep and before the Creator Forward, out of the coin's own collected earnings only, so a forward is never negative and a share too small waits; each withholding is a `graduation_fee` Ledger entry on the graduation's signature from event index 1000, never added to a revenue total (the money arrived as a `platform_fee` or `pool_fee`), and the Creators' share the sweep and the budgets subtract is net of it.
  Nothing moves on-chain and nothing ever comes out of a wallet or the holders' Reward Tax.
- Fee figures in one place (owner, 2026-10-30): `/fees` is the only page that shows a percentage or a rate, every one read from `GET /v1/platform` and `GET /v1/burns` at render; every other page says what a fee is for and links there ("Every fee, explained").
  A visitor's own amounts stay where the trade is: the trade panel's one Fees line in the asset paid, the launch review's first-buy share and its Graduation Fee note.
  The Stats page (`/revenue`, the header's Stats) is the Platform Token's page in the shape of pump.fun's coin page from our data (section 12 of `docs/design/principles.md`); `/burns` stays under it.
- After Graduation a trader pays 1.25% and the platform collects about 1.16% of it: 95% of the pool's 1% creator fee (mainnet's config keeps 5% of it for Raydium, its `creator_fee_share_rate`, found when the local chain took that config on 2026-10-25; half of what arrives is forwarded to the Creator on the first Creator Tier, so the Creator's exact share after Graduation starts at 0.475%, said as about 0.5% everywhere but the launch summary's note, owner's choice; 0.57% and 0.665% on the second and the top tier, ADR 0020) and, through the Fee Key on the locked liquidity, the liquidity providers' 84% of the 0.25% trade fee; Raydium keeps 0.04%.
  The mainnet CPMM config is recorded in the runbook's section 2. StonkFun's own token trades mostly on Meteora, but its launchpad is LaunchLab like ours.

## Rewards and budgets

- Rewards (owner, 2026-11-09, recorded on #187): no Graduation grant or any reward paid in STOKER to Creators (they could sell the platform's own coin); Creator Earnings gain a liquidity destination (built, the Creator Earnings Destinations bullet below); the Creator's share ladder only ever climbs (50% the floor the owner picked over 25% on 2026-11-09; built as #188, the bullet below); every new program (rebates, referrals on every pair, an integrator share) is a row of the Budget Table, never a share taken on its own; Airdrop Mode (#49) stays parked while tokenized-stock holders on Solana are few.
- The Budget Table (#187, ADR 0019, owner 2026-11-09): the Platform Net (the Ledger's `platform_fee`, `pool_fee` and `fee_key` entries less the Creators' share, Fee Key collections whole) is divided by one table of six rows, `burn`, `flywheel`, `rebates`, `referrals`, `integrators` and `treasury`, in basis points summing to 10000, which the contract and a Postgres check both enforce; version 1 is 60 / 5 / 0 / 0 / 0 / 35 and `BURN_SHARE_BPS` and `FLYWHEEL_SHARE_BPS` no longer exist.
  A version is announced by the operator with the fee change's lead time and after every earlier version, never edited, and its shares apply only to the net collected while it is in force: with `C(t)` the net from Ledger entries before `t` at the latest prices, a row is allotted the sum over versions of `(C(next start) - C(this start)) * share`, which for a share that never changed is the old formula to the lamport.
  The owner's plan: the trader programs are paid by the Burn alone (target Burn 40%, Flywheel 5%, programs 20% together, treasury 35%), a program's row stays 0 until it is built and running (a share without a spender only fills the hot wallet), and the three together never pass `TRADER_PROGRAMS_MAX_BPS` (2000), which the API refuses.
  A program built later adds its row's Ledger kind to `SPENT_KIND` in `budget.ts`, or its spending is never counted.
- Volume rebates (#196, ADR 0021, owner 2026-11-12, seven answers before the build): every trade earns, curve and pool, buys and sells, by the Platform Fee it paid; a Rebate Week is a UTC week from Monday 00:00, settled once after it ends (`REBATE_WEEK_SECONDS` shortens it on a test stack alone: 30 seconds in the backend suite, 60 in the UI suite); the week's pot is what the `rebates` row has left less the rebates still to be paid, split in proportion to the fees paid; paid in SOL once a wallet's waiting rebates reach 0.01 SOL (`REBATE_MIN_LAMPORTS`), a share under it waits and is never returned; the first share to announce is 10% from the Burn (Burn 50, Flywheel 5, rebates 10, treasury 35), the operator's step.
  Three calls the session made and told the owner, theirs to overrule: the cap is the row's own share of the fees paid (a week's pot is never more than 10% of the week's fees with the row at 10%), tighter than the fees paid, since a Creator trading their own coin also earns the Creator's share; a trade earns only while the row has a share in force, so a first week starts on a Monday 00:00 UTC; the admin wallet earns nothing beside the operations wallet and the treasury.
  A week with earning time and no trades is still a row, with nothing given.
  The Rewards page shows the program (`VolumeRebates.tsx`, with `YourRebates.tsx` for the connected wallet) and hides the wallet panel and the weeks until a share is announced; the figures are on `/fees`.
  `budget-table.test.ts` and `rebates.test.ts` both announce Budget Tables on the shared backend stack and run in either order, so each counts from the table it finds.
- Holding Weight (#199, ADR 0022, owner 2026-11-12, seven answers before the build), shown to holders as Diamond Hands: in every Reward Round, Reward Tax rounds and Holder Dividends alike, a coin kept longer counts for more, coin by coin (each coin's own age, never the wallet's); the owner's first weights are 1.5 times from 7 days and twice from 30, changeable by an announced rules version; a sell sets back only the coins sold, newest first; coins moved between wallets arrive as new coins; the treasury stays eligible and always counts once.
  The Reward Engine looks once a Holding Day (a UTC day, `HOLDING_DAY_SECONDS`, shortened on a test stack alone and refused on mainnet) at every coin that can have a round and brings its Holding Lots (`holding_lots`, with one `holding_checks` row a day) in step with the balances; a round's snapshot reads the lots and never writes them, takes them oldest first up to the balance, and counts whatever is beyond them once; a payout is `pot * weightedHolding / weightedTotal`, rounded down, and with no steps it is the earlier payout to the unit.
  Nothing is weighted until the operator announces a rules version with steps (runbook section 18: 7 days at 15000 and 30 days at 20000, which can ride with the owed version 2 of section 6); the checks run from the deploy, so ages have run for the lead time by then, and every announcement names `holdingWeights`, the empty list included.
  Three calls the session made and told the owner, theirs to overrule: balances are looked at once a day and not at every run of the engine; a wallet under the minimum holding keeps its coins' ages; a version carries at most six steps and none above five times.
  The Rewards page shows the rule (`DiamondHandsRules.tsx`) only while a version with steps is in force or announced, a coin's rewards cards show the connected wallet's own lots (`DiamondHands.tsx`, from `GET /v1/launches/:mint/holdings/:wallet`), and a round's page shows each payout's weight once any payout counts for more than once.
  Left as it was and the owner's to reword: the Rewards page's lead still says the tax is paid "pro rata" and the earnings card "by the size of its holding", each with the Diamond Hands note or block beside it.
- Reward Rounds and the Burn's budgets: `docs/adr/0006-reward-rounds-and-burn-budgets.md`.
  Burn and Flywheel budgets are shares of the Ledger's revenue, net of the Creators' share (ADR 0009), less what earlier burns spent; the Fee Collector's sweep leaves the unspent budgets in the operations wallet.
  A payout's own Reward Tax is withheld again, so a paid round can open the next one.
- The Payout Asset (#43, ADR 0006 amendment): a Reward Launch pays its holders in the coin or in its Quote Asset, chosen on the launch form, kept in `launch_drafts.reward_payout`, copied to `launches` by the Indexer (a Reward Launch made without our API pays in the coin), never updated.
  For a Quote Asset payout the Reward Engine sells one slice per run into the coin's own market, at most `REWARD_SALE_MAX_RESERVE_BPS` (0.5%) of its coin reserve with a 1% slippage limit (`apps/api/src/rewards/sales.ts`), writes each sale to `reward_sales` before sending, records it as two `reward_sale` Ledger entries and indexes it as a trade; a round opens once the coins sold since the last round reach the rules' threshold and pays the pot (landed proceeds less what was paid) in the Quote Asset, SOL as plain SOL.
  The sweep leaves SOL pots behind (`rewardPotLamports`); token pots are never swept.
  A SOL payout under the rent minimum to a wallet with no SOL is `returned` to the pot and the round finishes without it.
- The treasury is an eligible holder in Reward Rounds (owner, 2026-10-04): it holds coins swept from pool fees and takes its share, and the rewards page says so.
  It is a plain file keypair (not a hardware wallet, owner 2026-10-06), so `eligibleHolders` counts it; a program-owned vault would be skipped.
  Excluding it later is an announced rule change, only if the community asks.

## Platform, keys and policy

- The platform's keys (owner, 2026-10-06; prefix shortened 2026-10-09): admin, Treasury, operations wallet and the Platform Token's mint are four file keypairs whose addresses start with `Stoke` (a prefix, visible in truncated addresses, and the brand's verb; `Stoker` was the plan until the grind was measured at 18 days a key, see the trap below, and a suffix as well is computationally out of reach), ground on the owner's Mac into `~/.config/stoker/mainnet/` (runbook section 1 has the command, the log and the rate; all four landed on 2026-10-10 and were assigned on 2026-10-13: admin `Stoke7675...`, operations `StokehV2b...`, treasury `StokezuRE...`, Platform Token mint `StokeDgBj...`, the full addresses in the runbook's section 1 and on #23; the mainnet PlatformConfig and Platform Lookup Table were created the same day, so go-live items 2 to 4 are done and the plain keypair files leave the Mac after the API deploy).
  Launches are open to anyone from the first deploy: no gate (owner, 2026-10-14, #97 closed), and the platform goes live after 2026-10-25.
  The Platform Token launches through the chosen-mint-key path (#75, merged: `POST /v1/operator/mint-keys`, runbook section 10); every other coin keeps a derived address until #73.
- The region gate (owner, 2026-10-29; ADR 0014): nothing is withheld upfront anywhere; every feature works in every country and the disclosure does its job; a feature is removed from one country only when someone (a regulator, say) asks for it there, by a row in the operator's region-gates table (`PUT /v1/operator/region-gates`, the operator view's "Region gates"), never by a deploy and never ahead of a request.
  Engagement patterns are never refused on research grounds (owner, 2026-10-28).
- The disclaimer's excluded list (US, UK, Canada, sanctioned countries) is confirmed by the owner; the words are `EXCLUDED_REGIONS` in `apps/web/src/lib/legal.ts`, one source for every page.
  Since #156 (owner, 2026-10-30, after pump.fun's welcome as a shape only) the first visit shows the welcome: our words, one Continue, the terms under it, accepted once per browser; it blocks nothing, so Escape and a press outside accept too.
  The launch form's "Holder rewards, Coming soon" card of 2026-10-30 is gone since #113 was built (2026-11-10): it is the "Your holders" choice of the form's Creator earnings section.
- Chart marks (#160, ADR 0018, owner 2026-11-09): the trade marks alone are live; the mentions (posts on X found through twitterapi.io, a paid search) stay built but off, since `TWITTERAPI_API_KEY` is left unset on every Worker, and are switched on only if the community asks for them.
  Never set that key from a session, never remove the mentions code, and never replace the search with another paid source without the owner's word.
- Row-Level Security (#201, 2026-11-13): every table has it, with no policy and never forced, since migration `0030`.
  The API reaches Postgres through Hyperdrive as the tables' owner, whom it does not apply to, so nothing of ours changed; every other role reads and writes nothing, which is what closes Supabase's Data API over the same schema whether its dashboard switch is off (it is, in both projects, since 2026-11-12) or ever on again.
  A new table is declared with `.enableRLS()`; the lint (`scripts/row-security.mjs`) and `runMigrations` both refuse one without it.
  A policy is a decision to open a table to another role and none is planned: the site and every client read through the API alone.
